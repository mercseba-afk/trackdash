# Release rarity policy

Decision: 2026-10-29. Keep a single public rarity classification, describing the
collector scarcity of the exact physical edition. Do not add explanatory labels
or a separate market-availability classification to the interface.

## Classification

| Stored value | Italian label | Required basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common | Comune | Broad distribution and routine availability of the exact edition from independent sellers or normal retail stock. Discontinuation alone does not change this class. |
| Uncommon | Non comune | The exact edition remains obtainable, but supply is intermittent or distribution is narrower. A special colour or an older year alone is insufficient. |
| Rare | Rara | Sustained difficulty sourcing the exact edition, supported by repeated observations across independent relevant sources; documented restricted distribution or historical edition evidence must corroborate the assessment. |
| Very Rare | Molto rara | Exceptional, sustained scarcity plus strong evidence of a restricted run, distribution, or physically distinguishable historical variant. Requires stronger evidence than Rare, not merely a high asking price. |

Do not assign Grail: desirability is not a scarcity tier. Do not add it to the
normal four-level scale. Do not convert existing records in bulk under this policy.

## Evidence and reassessment

- Evaluate the exact release, not the product family or Item Number alone.
  Confirm packaging, chassis and other documented discriminators when numbers
  are shared across original runs and reissues.
- Compare complete, unbuilt kits when judging kit scarcity. Exclude spare parts,
  assembled examples, duplicates and listings with ambiguous release identity.
- Assess the relevant international market, including Japan for Mini4WD.
  Absence from Italian shops alone is insufficient evidence of rarity.
- For a new Rare/Very Rare assessment, retain observations on at least three
  dates spanning at least 90 days, across at least two independent relevant
  sources, together with edition/distribution documentation. These are initial
  editorial evidence requirements, not an established industry standard.
- Failed scans, unconfigured searches, missing source coverage and zero matches
  do not establish scarcity. Record evidence and assessment dates internally;
  add no new public explanatory badges.
- Price, demand, sale volume, age and discontinued status do not independently
  determine rarity. Keep production status and market valuations separate.
- Insufficient evidence means no new classification. Existing labels remain
  legacy assessments until individually reviewed; this document does not certify
  that all existing classifications meet the new requirements.
- Review against accumulated evidence, including confirmed reissues/restocking;
  never change rarity automatically on a daily scanner refresh.

## Examples, not automatic assignments

- A 1988 original can be Rare when its original physical identity and sustained
  scarcity are evidenced. Age alone cannot justify Rare or Very Rare.
- A 2006 release can be Common when routinely available, Uncommon with
  intermittent supply, or Rare when the stronger criteria above are met.
- A recently discontinued release stays Common while plentiful. Reassess only
  when evidence supports a different tier.
