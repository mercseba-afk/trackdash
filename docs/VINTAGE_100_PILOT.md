# TrackDash Vintage 100 Pilot

Status: P1 listing-level validation in progress

## Goal

Measure whether the collectible/vintage Mini 4WD market is liquid enough to support TrackDash Price Intelligence at exact Release level. The first execution block is a **Vintage Core 25**; expand to 50 and then 100 only after the first block produces usable evidence.

## Non-negotiable evidence rules

- Unit of analysis is the exact **Release**, never the generic model family.
- Target condition for the pilot: **new / complete / unbuilt**.
- SOLD, ASK and RETAIL remain separate evidence classes.
- Do not use mixed lots, body-only/parts, built cars, customized cars or obvious incomplete kits as exact-release sale comps.
- Reissues, Premium, MS/VS/Super-II variants, Black/Special editions and modern rereleases are separate Releases and must never be merged into the original.
- When the same Tamiya Item Number is reused across commercial occurrences, Item Number alone is insufficient: require period/edition evidence (box, chassis, listing wording such as 当時物/初版, verified date, barcode when available, or equivalent provenance).
- A broad marketplace search count is discovery evidence only; it is not an exact-release sales count until individual results are filtered.
- Public search snippets are candidate evidence only. Do not write them into R3 until the exact listing/release match, condition, source date and currency provenance are validated.
- A historical sale may support valuation history while being too old to support **current liquidity**. Liquidity must retain its own recency gate.
- If exact-release evidence is insufficient, fail closed rather than inventing a Market Value or liquidity level.

## P1 scan fields

For each exact Release collect:

- official identity: item number, exact edition, year/date, chassis, known rereleases/variants that can contaminate searches;
- eBay Product Research: exact sold comps, ideally multiple query variants and last 3 years;
- Yahoo Auctions Japan closed sales: exact clean sold comps, with individual listing filtering;
- current active asks from eBay/Japan marketplaces where legally/technically available;
- retail only when the exact Release is genuinely still purchasable;
- clean sold count by explicit period;
- min / median / max sold price after exclusions;
- most recent exact sale date;
- average/median gap between clean sales when enough observations exist;
- observed ask/sold spread;
- provenance and exclusion reason for every rejected ambiguous comp.

## Early interpretation — not valuation

- The P1 set already shows meaningful price stratification: vintage collecting is not a flat market.
- **18012** currently looks like the clearest high-ticket vintage signal.
- **18031** currently looks like the clearest repeat-sale/liquidity signal in the recent Yahoo window.
- **18034** has developed a convincing repeat cluster around JPY 10k–11k.
- **18038** looks strong, but its shared item number makes provenance stricter than for ordinary releases.
- 18023 demonstrates why historical sold evidence and current liquidity must not be conflated.
- A Release with one high sale must not outrank a lower-priced Release with repeat recent sales solely because of ticket value.
- eBay Product Research remains important for cross-market confirmation before publication of pilot-derived Market Value/trend/liquidity.

## Expansion rule

After P1, rank candidates by:

1. exact clean sold volume;
2. recency / continuity of sales;
3. collectible ticket value;
4. price dispersion and condition sensitivity;
5. evidence quality across independent source families.

Only then expand Core 25 toward Vintage/Collector 50 and 100. The final 100 may include later limited/special releases when collector relevance is stronger than age alone.
